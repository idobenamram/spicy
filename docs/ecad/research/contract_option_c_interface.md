## Summary

In this option **the contract lives on the ports**. Every port says two things. First, **what it accepts** from the world: the source on an input (its signal, its impedance, its ripple, droop and faults), the load on an output (its resistance, capacitance, current and steps), the ambient temperature. Second, **what it presents** to the world: its own input impedance, output impedance, voltage level and current draw. These are the same two halves that a digital timing library (Liberty: delay characterized over input slew × output load), an IBIS buffer model and a datasheet header use. The word `assume` disappears. `accepts` replaces it, on the port it concerns, and the port's role (input or output) says whether the thing accepted is a source or a load. **Specs hold over everything a block accepts.** **Setups are derived:** the accepted environment *is* the default bench, swept over its ranges, and each named event in it (a load step, a VBUS droop, a cable pulled out) is a stimulus a spec can select with `under`, `during` or `after`. Hand-written benches remain for the few cases the environment can't express (a datasheet row outside the operating range, a loop broken for loop gain, a raw SPICE fixture), and even those keep the port monitors that check them. **Composition is a check at every net, in both directions:** what the driver presents must fit what each receiver accepts, and what the receivers present (their impedance, their summed current, their emitted steps) must fit what the driver accepts. When all checks pass, a child's verdicts carry over to the parent without simulation, and cached port tables (two-port matrices, keyed by the child's definition, never by where it's placed) make linear board specs cheap. Written out in full, the reference board of [EE] §4 has 7 blocks, the 10 board specs of [EE] §4.5 and three fault specs. Two of the 10 (USB capacitance and current) are generated by the USB environment itself. At the corners simulated here, the cable-unplug fault shows a hold-up of at least 245 µs and a power-fail warning that comes at least 103 µs before the ADC reading goes bad **[S]**. The option has three weak spots. Impedance *shapes* must match for a containment check to be decidable. Declared ranges add knobs to every block bench. And board-only specs about shared rails still need flat simulation.

## 1. The philosophy and its concepts

### 1.1 The philosophy in one paragraph

A block is a thing with ports, and everything it can be held to happens at a port. So the contract is written **port by port**, like a datasheet's pin table plus its "test conditions unless otherwise noted" header. For each port the author writes the **environment it accepts** (the header: "VIN 4.3–5.5 V from a source of ≤ 0.5 Ω with ≤ 100 mVpp ripple; the load draws 0.1–50 mA and may step 5 → 30 mA in 1 µs") and the **characteristics it presents** ("Z_out ≤ 1 Ω for I ≥ 5 mA; V = 3.3 V ± 2%"). Behavioral specs (gain, PSRR, settling) sit beside the ports and hold **for everything accepted**. Nothing about the test setup is written twice. The setup is the environment. Every accepted range becomes a knob, every accepted disturbance an excitation, every accepted event a transient stimulus, every accepted fault a scenario. Composition is where the payoff comes. The environment one block accepts is exactly what its neighbour must present, so a parent proves a child's assumptions by comparing two declarations per net, both ways. Timing sign-off works the same way: cells are characterized once over their accepted input slew and output load, and the tool checks that every real net stays inside the table (`max_capacitance`, `max_transition`).

### 1.2 The words

| Word | Where | Meaning | Replaces / precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| `accepts` | on a port, or for an ambient quantity | The environment the block tolerates. **For all** of it, every spec must hold. **Wherever the block is placed**, the neighbours must stay inside it | `assume` (both of its jobs, [refs] §18.3); edg `voltage_limits`; datasheet "recommended operating conditions" |
| `presents` | on a port | A guarantee about the port itself: `z_in`, `z_out`, `v`, `i`, `c`, or a load/source *shape*. Checked by derived characterization setups, and published for composition | v0.1 had no home for the block's own `z_in` / `z_out` ([EE] §5 item 2); Liberty pin capacitance; IBIS tables |
| `spec` | in the contract | A behavioral guarantee between ports (gain, PSRR, settling), held over the accepted environment | unchanged; still the centre |
| `event` | inside a port's environment | A normal-operation stimulus the environment may apply: a load step, a line step, a droop, a power-up ramp | v0.1 form benches with `Load::Step`, TLV755P Figs. 5-7/5-8 |
| `fault` | inside a port's environment | An abnormal event: the cable opens, the connector is reversed, a pin shorts | new (G19, P15) |
| `emits` | on a child's port | An event the block itself causes on its neighbours (the MCU waking up draws a current step) | new; the dual of `event` |
| `under` / `during` / `after` | at the end of a spec | Which member of the environment the spec's setup applies. `under` for disturbances and events, `during` / `after` for faults | CRML's `during … ensure`; P15 item 3 |
| `where` | at the end of a spec or `presents` | Pins environment members to one value, or narrows them, **inside** what the port accepts (a datasheet row condition) | v0.1 `where` |
| `bench` | in the contract | A one-off setup: overrides of the environment (`..env`), or a raw SPICE fixture. Going outside the accepted environment needs `#[outside(reason)]` | v0.1 form / sheet bench |
| `environment` | top-level item | A named, reusable port environment (USB 2.0 low-power port, a 74LVC input, a 600 Ω line) | new; IBIS model reuse, datasheet standards |
| `requires` | inside an `environment` | What the environment demands of any port that accepts it (USB: ≤ 10 µF, ≤ 100 mA). It becomes an automatic spec | USB 2.0 §7.2; Liberty `max_capacitance` seen from the other side |

**What replaces `assume`:** `accepts`, written *on the port it concerns*. The two jobs `assume` did at once are now visible in one word. For the block's own check, the accepted range is a knob. For the parent, it is an obligation. The role still decides legality (v0.1 §8.2): an input port accepts a `Source` or a `Supply`, an output port accepts a `Load`, and writing `accepts` for something the port produces is an error, just as `assume output.v` is today.

### 1.3 Three kinds of range, three visibly different places

[refs] §18.1 item 5 found that datasheets hide three different kinds of condition in one column. Here each kind has its own place:

| Written | Kind | Quantifier | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `port vin accepts Supply { v: 4.3V..=5.5V }` | **condition range** (a knob) | for all | the LDO over every VIN |
| `spec psrr: … where vin.v == 4.3V, vout.i == 50mA` | **fixed point** (a datasheet row) | at this value, which must lie inside the accepted range | TLV755P PSRR condition |
| `dc(vout.v).span(vin.v)` | **measure axis** (swept inside one measure) | over the axis | line regulation |
| `value: 470k ± 1%` | **spread** (a part knob) | for all (statistical) | unchanged from v0.1 |
| `presents z_in: 450kΩ..` / `spec gain: … in 10.09 ± 3%` | **limit** | ⊆ | unchanged |

### 1.4 Shapes: what an environment is made of

An environment is a small typed value. The std library defines the shapes, in the language itself (v0.1 §3.3):

```rust
// std/env.spl (sketch)
pub struct Supply { v: Volt, z_src: Impedance, ripple: Ripple, noise: Noise }  // a rail's source: Thévenin V + Z
pub struct Source { signal: Signal, z_src: Impedance, offset: Volt }            // a signal's source: its EMF + Z
pub struct Load   { r: Ohm, c: Farad, i: Amp }                                   // parallel R ∥ C ∥ current sink
pub mod Load {
    pub struct Iso     { r: Ohm, c: Farad }                  // R in series, then C to ground (an isolated capacitive load)
    pub struct Sampled { r_sw: Ohm, c_s: Farad, f_s: Hertz, t_s: Second, prev: Volt, leak: Amp }  // SAR ADC input
    pub struct Model<B> {}                                   // "the load is that block's input"
}
pub enum Impedance { R(Ohm), Rl { r: Ohm, l: Henry }, Iso { r: Ohm, c: Farad } }  // a bare Ohm range means R
pub struct Ripple { vpp: Volt, f: Hertz }
pub enum Signal { Dc(Volt), Sine { amp: Volt, freq: Hertz }, Band { .. }, Diff { dm: Signal, cm: Signal } }
pub struct Step { from: Quantity, to: Quantity, by: Quantity, edge: Second, hold: Second }
pub struct Ramp { from: Volt, rise: Second }
pub struct Open { edge: Second, for: Second }                // a contact opens, and closes again after `for`
pub struct Swap { with: PortRef }                            // two conductors exchanged
pub struct Short { to: PortRef }
```

Each shape knows three things:
1. **how it lowers to SPICE** (a `Supply` is a V source plus a series R or R+L; `Load::Sampled` is a switch, a capacitor reset to `prev` and a clock);
2. **which of its fields are knobs** (every range);
3. **how containment is decided** against another shape (§3.2).

A shape may also offer measures. `Load::Sampled` offers `sample_error()`, the error on C_s at the end of each acquisition. `Source` gives the port an `.emf`, the source's open-circuit voltage, so an **EMF-referred** gain (`ac(out / sensor.emf)`) and a **pin-to-pin** gain (`ac(out / input.v)`) are two different, visible things ([EE] §1.4).

### 1.5 Interface types carry default environments, and nothing is hidden

Signal types and interfaces may name a default environment:

```rust
// std/signals.spl (sketch, extending v0.1 §3.3)
pub signal Power<R: Role>  { source: Supply, load: Load,   default: none }   // undeclared = ideal, flagged
pub signal Analog<R: Role> { source: Source, load: Load,   default: none }
pub signal Logic<R: Role>  { source: Edge,   load: Load,   default: family } // levels, edge rate and C_L from the part family (74LVC: ≤ 10 ns/V, 15 pF)
pub interface Usb2<R: UsbRole> {
    vbus: Power<R>, dp: Logic<InOut>, dm: Logic<InOut>, gnd: Ground,
    default(Device) { vbus: usb::Usb2LowPower }    // a device must work on the weakest compliant port
}
```

A default is a convenience that could hide what is tested. The rules that stop it:

| Risk | Rule |
|---|---|
| A default applies silently | **`spicy fmt` writes it out.** A port that picks up its type's default gets the line `port vbus accepts Usb2LowPower;` inserted by the formatter, like Cargo writing `edition`. The text always ends up saying it by name |
| A named environment hides its numbers | The I/O page and `spicy env <block>` print the **resolved header** (every field, with where it came from), datasheet style. `spicy fmt --expand` inlines it |
| A port with no environment | It gets the **ideal** one (a 1 V AC source, an open output, an exact supply), which is v0.1's default bench, so the MVP stays simple. But every verdict's claim says so ("output: open, not declared"), and composition refuses to reuse that verdict without an in-context run (§3.2) |
| An accepted disturbance nobody tests | **Coverage:** the report lists environment members that no spec runs under ("vin.ripple accepted; no spec is `under vin.ripple`"). The environment is a checklist, so G14 comes almost for free |
| A bench that drifts outside the environment | **Port monitors** (§4.2) read the actual port voltage and current in every run of every setup, derived or hand-written, and compare them with `accepts`. A hand-written bench can't quietly leave the envelope |
| A library environment changes under you | Named environments are versioned with the library, and a version change marks every dependent verdict `stale` (its hash is in `rev`) |
| "Which setup produced this number?" | Every derived setup has a stable name (`Ldo3v3::vout.load_step/tran`) shown in the verdict line and viewable as a generated deck (`spicy deck Ldo3v3 --setup vout.load_step`) |

### 1.6 How setups are derived

```
   contract (ports)                         derived setups (one engine deck each)
   ─────────────────                        ──────────────────────────────────────
   port vin  accepts Supply{v, z_src,       env/dc      V(vin.v)+R(z_src) · I(vout.i)+C(vout.c)   op, DC sweeps
                 ripple}                    env/ac(vin) same + AC 1 V on the vin source            AC   ← psrr, "ac(vout.v / vin.v)"
             event line_step                vin.line_step/tran   PWL step on the vin source        tran
             event power_up                 vin.power_up/tran    ramp from 0 V                     tran
   port vout accepts Load{i, c}             presents/z_out   excitation off, 1 A AC into vout      AC   ← presents z_out
             event load_step                vout.load_step/tran  current sink with a PULSE step    tran
             presents z_out, v              bench dropout_row    (hand-written one-off)           DC sweep
```

**The rule.** A spec's setup is the tuple (block, the static environment, the excitation, the events it names, the analysis family). The excitation comes from the spec itself: the port in the denominator of an AC ratio (`ac(vout.v / vin.v)` excites `vin`), or the member named by `under`. Specs with the same tuple share one deck and one run table. So the static specs of a block (DC level, gain, bandwidth) share one setup, which is the datasheet's "most specifications use Figure 1-1" ([EE] §2.2). Each event adds one transient setup, and each `presents` quantity has a standard characterization setup (current injection for `z_out`, a current probe for `z_in`).

## 2. The reference board, written in full

The board of [EE] §4: USB 5 V → cable → TLV755P-class LDO → 10 Ω + 10 µF VDDA filter → MCP6001 gain stage (G = 10.09) → 470 Ω / 100 nF anti-alias filter → STM32F103 ADC, with an MCU load of 5–30 mA stepping on the 3.3 V rail. Values, ranges and bounds are those of [EE] §4.3–4.5 unless a comment says otherwise.

### 2.1 The USB source and cable: an environment, not a block

In this option the USB host and its cable aren't parts of the board. They are **the environment of the board's `vbus` port**, published once in the std library, from the standard.

```rust
// std/usb.spl
/// USB 2.0 §7.2, a low-power (bus-powered hub) downstream port, seen at the device's receptacle through its cable.
pub environment Usb2LowPower for Power<In> {
    accepts Supply {
        v: 4.40V..=5.25V,                                 // §7.2.2, Table 7-7 (open-circuit, at the host)
        z_src: 0.1Ω..=0.5Ω,                               // VBUS + GND wires and contacts
        ripple: Ripple { vpp: ..=100mV, f: 100Hz..=1MHz }, // assumed: USB gives no ripple number
    };
    event droop:  Step { by: -330mV, edge: 1us, hold: 200us };  // §7.2.4.2: another device plugged into the hub
    event attach: Ramp { from: 0V, rise: 100us };               // hot-plug, as the cable limits it
    fault unplug:   Open { edge: 1us, for: 10us..=5ms };        // the cable is pulled (or bounces), then comes back
    fault reversed: Swap { with: gnd };                         // a miswired cable or harness
    requires c <= 10uF;                                         // §7.2.4.1: capacitance on VBUS at attach
    requires i <= 100mA;                                        // §7.2.1: one unit load before configuration
}

/// USB 2.0 high-power port: the same, with a narrower voltage and 500 mA after configuration.
pub environment Usb2HighPower for Power<In> {
    accepts Supply { v: 4.75V..=5.25V, ..Usb2LowPower };
    requires i <= 500mA;
    ..Usb2LowPower
}
```

The electrical reasoning, for the reader who isn't an engineer. A USB host is a 5 V supply that the standard allows to be as low as 4.40 V. The cable's two wires (VBUS and ground) have about 0.1–0.5 Ω of resistance together, so the board sees the host *through a resistor*, and its voltage sags when it draws current. That resistor is the `z_src` of the supply. Ripple, droop and the ramp at plug-in are all properties of what *arrives*. They belong to the source ([EE] §1.6). So does a cable pulled out: a fault is just an extreme event of the source.

### 2.2 `Ldo3v3`, with the one-off setup

```rust
/// 5 V → 3.3 V linear regulator, TLV755P-class, with its input and output capacitors.
pub block Ldo3v3 {
    port vin: Power<In>;
    port vout: Power<Out>;
    port gnd: Ground;
    let u1    = Tlv755p { vin, vout, gnd };                       // subcircuit part; V_ref ±1% is its knob
    let c_in  = Capacitor { a: vin,  b: gnd, value: 1uF ± 10% };
    let c_out = Capacitor { a: vout, b: gnd, value: 1uF ± 10%, esr: 5mΩ };
}

contract Ldo3v3 {
    accepts temp: -40°C..=125°C;                  // rated T_J; must contain the project's range

    port vin {
        accepts Supply { v: 4.3V..=5.5V, z_src: ..=0.5Ω, ripple: Ripple { vpp: ..=100mV, f: 100Hz..=1MHz } };
        event line_step: Step { by: +1V, edge: 1us };               // TLV755P Fig. 5-7
        event power_up:  Ramp { from: 0V, rise: 100us };
        presents i: ..=(vout.i + 50uA);                             // ground current 25 µA typ.
        presents c: 1uF ± 10%;                                      // C_in, for the USB capacitance budget
    }
    port vout {
        accepts Load { i: 0.1mA..=50mA, c: ..=20uF };
        event load_step: Step { i: 5mA -> 30mA, edge: 1us };        // [EE] §4.4
        /// Output accuracy over every accepted VIN, load and V_ref.
        presents v: 3.3V ± 2%;                                      // [EE] 3.2967–3.3013 V (nominal V_ref)
        /// Light loads make the output impedance peak (3.2 Ω at 1 mA), so the guarantee starts at 5 mA.
        presents z_out: ..=1Ω over 10Hz..=1MHz where vout.i >= 5mA;
        #[declared(datasheet = "TLV755P SBVS320D §5.5")]
        presents i_max: 500mA..;                                    // not modeled: taken from the datasheet
    }

    spec line_reg: dc(vout.v).span(vin.v) <= 5mV where vout.i == 30mA;            // [EE] 1.0 mV
    spec load_reg: dc(vout.v).slope(vout.i) >= -0.1V/A;                           // [EE] 56 mV/A
    spec psrr: ac(vout.v / vin.v).at(100kHz).db() <= -36dB where vin.v == 4.3V, vout.i == 50mA;   // [EE] 40.0 dB
    spec dip:  tran(vout.v).deviation() <= 50mV under vout.load_step;              // [EE] −19.9 / +16.4 mV
    spec line_tr: tran(vout.v).deviation() <= 20mV under vin.line_step;           // [EE] +9.3 / −8.3 mV
    spec start: tran(vout.v).crossing(rising 3.2V).since(vin.power_up) <= 1ms under vin.power_up;  // [EE] 385 µs
    spec no_overshoot: tran(vout.v).max() <= 3.4V under vin.power_up;             // [EE] 3.299 V

    /// ONE-OFF: the datasheet's dropout row. It is defined at the full 500 mA and below the operating VIN,
    /// both outside what this block accepts in an application, so it is a bench, not a `where`.
    bench dropout_row {
        #[outside(reason = "datasheet row: dropout is defined at the rated 500 mA, below the operating VIN")]
        vout: Load { i: 500mA },
        vin: Supply { v: Sweep(4.5V -> 3.0V), z_src: 0Ω },
        ..env
    }
    let v_drop = sweep(vin.v).first(vout.v < 0.99 * 3.3V);
    spec dropout: v_drop - 0.99 * 3.3V <= 250mV on dropout_row;                   // [EE] 215 mV
}
```

What the one-off shows:
- `..env` means "everything else as accepted". The temperature, V_ref and C_out knobs still apply, so the row is checked over tolerances, unlike a datasheet row.
- `#[outside]` is required because the bench leaves the accepted environment. Without it the port monitors would flag the bench (§1.5).
- A verdict from an outside bench is tagged `characterization`. It is **never** used for composition: a parent can't rely on behaviour outside what the block accepts.

### 2.3 `VddaFilter`

```rust
/// RC filter that keeps the MCU's digital noise off the analog rail (ST AN2834).
pub block VddaFilter {
    port vin: Power<In>; port vout: Power<Out>; port gnd: Ground;
    let r = Resistor  { a: vin,  b: vout, value: 10Ω ± 1% };
    let c = Capacitor { a: vout, b: gnd,  value: 10uF ± 10% };
}

contract VddaFilter {
    port vin {
        accepts Supply { v: 3.2V..=3.4V, z_src: ..=1Ω };
        presents i: vout.i;                                   // a series element: what goes out comes in
        presents z_in: Load::Iso { r: 10Ω ± 1%, c: 10uF ± 10% };   // what the LDO sees: 10 µF behind 10 Ω
    }
    port vout {
        accepts Load { i: ..=1mA, c: ..=1uF };
        presents v: vin.v - (0mV..=10.1mV);                   // a propagated guarantee: a function of what arrives
        presents z_out: ..=10.2Ω over ..=1MHz;
    }
    /// 1.6 kHz corner: attenuates the MCU's steps and the rail's ripple before they reach V_REF.
    spec atten: ac(vout.v / vin.v).at(100kHz).db() <= -30dB;      // [C] −35 dB at C −10%, R −1%
}
```

`presents v: vin.v - (0mV..=10.1mV)` is a **propagated guarantee**. The filter can't promise an absolute voltage, only "what arrives, minus at most 10.1 mV" (1 mA × 10.1 Ω). The parent substitutes what actually arrives. This is how a Liberty cell's output slew is a function of its input slew: guarantees can depend on the accepted environment.

### 2.4 `GainStage` (placing `MidRef`)

```rust
/// Mid-rail bias: vmid = vdd / 2, high impedance.
pub block MidRef {
    port vdd: Power<In>; port mid: Analog<Out>; port gnd: Ground;
    let r_m1  = Resistor  { a: vdd, b: mid, value: 100k ± 1% };
    let r_m2  = Resistor  { a: mid, b: gnd, value: 100k ± 1% };
    let c_mid = Capacitor { a: mid, b: gnd, value: 1uF ± 10% };
}
contract MidRef {
    port vdd accepts Supply { v: 3.2V..=3.4V };
    port mid { accepts Load { r: 400kΩ.. }; presents v: vdd.v * (0.5 ± 1%); }   // [C] two ±1% resistors move the ratio ±1%
}

/// AC-coupled non-inverting op-amp stage, G = 1 + R_f/R_g = 10.09, biased at mid-rail.
pub block GainStage {
    port vdd: Power<In>; port input: Analog<In>; port output: Analog<Out>; port gnd: Ground;
    net inp; net inn; net ng; net vmid;
    let mid  = MidRef { vdd, mid: vmid, gnd };
    let c_in = Capacitor { a: input, b: inp, value: 220nF ± 10% };
    let r_b  = Resistor  { a: inp, b: vmid, value: 470k ± 1% };     // this is the stage's input impedance
    let u2   = Mcp6001   { inp, inn, vdd, vss: gnd, out: output };  // V_OS ±4.5 mV is its knob
    let r_f  = Resistor  { a: output, b: inn, value: 9.09k ± 1% };
    let r_g  = Resistor  { a: inn, b: ng, value: 1k ± 1% };
    let c_g  = Capacitor { a: ng, b: gnd, value: 47uF ± 20% };      // DC gain 1: the output sits at vmid
}

contract GainStage {
    port vdd {
        accepts Supply { v: 3.3V ± 3%, z_src: ..=20Ω, ripple: Ripple { vpp: ..=10mV, f: 10Hz..=1MHz } };
        presents i: ..=200uA;                                       // op-amp 100 µA + MidRef 16.5 µA, with margin
    }
    port input {
        accepts Source { signal: Sine { amp: ..=100mV, freq: 20Hz..=1kHz }, z_src: ..=10kΩ };
        presents z_in: 450kΩ.. over 20Hz..=1kHz;                    // [EE] 470–473 kΩ
    }
    port output {
        /// Either the isolated RC it was designed for, or a light resistive load. 100 nF direct would ring (69%).
        accepts Load::Iso { r: 400Ω..=1kΩ, c: ..=220nF } | Load { r: 10kΩ.., c: ..=1nF };
        /// Feedback makes the output impedance small but rising with frequency, like an inductor.
        presents z_out: Rl { r: ..=0.1Ω, l: ..=0.55mH } over ..=30kHz;   // [EE] 0.30 Ω @ 100 Hz, 3.0 Ω @ 1 kHz, 30 Ω @ 10 kHz
        presents v: 0.1V..=3.2V;                                    // the swing it guarantees
    }

    let h = ac(output.v / input.v);                                 // pin to pin: excites `input`
    spec gain:   h.at(1kHz).mag() in 10.09 ± 3%;                    // [EE] 10.089 (10 kΩ ∥ 60 pF), 10.106 (470 Ω + 100 nF)
    spec f_low:  h.f_low(-3dB) <= 5Hz;
    spec f_high: h.f_high(-3dB) >= 50kHz;                           // [EE] 60.6 kHz (Iso), 94.6 kHz (10 kΩ)
    spec swing:  tran(output.v).window(10ms..=30ms) in 0.1V..=3.2V; // over the accepted Sine: amp and freq are knobs
    spec psrr:   ac(output.v / vdd.v).band(10Hz..=1MHz).max().db() <= -60dB;   // [EE] 66 dB; excites `vdd`

    /// A step is a test method, not something the stage's environment does: a named bench.
    bench step_test { input: Source { signal: Step { by: 10mV, edge: 100ns }, z_src: 0Ω }, ..env }
    spec stable: tran(output.v).overshoot() <= 10% on step_test;    // [EE] 2.4% (470 Ω + 100 nF), 0.8% (1 nF)
}
```

`stable` shows the rule for named extra setups. The load range is still the environment (`..env`), so the step is applied into **every** accepted load. The bench adds only the stimulus the environment can't express. The union in `output accepts … | …` becomes two derived setups (one per load shape), and every spec's verdict is the worse of the two.

### 2.5 `AaFilter`

```rust
/// Anti-alias RC. Also the op-amp's isolation resistor, and the ADC's charge reservoir.
pub block AaFilter {
    port input: Analog<In>; port output: Analog<Out>; port gnd: Ground;
    let r_aa = Resistor  { a: input, b: output, value: 470Ω ± 1% };
    let c_aa = Capacitor { a: output, b: gnd,   value: 100nF ± 10% };
}

contract AaFilter {
    port input {
        accepts Source { z_src: Rl { r: ..=10Ω, l: ..=1mH } };       // an op-amp output, inductive
        presents z_in: Load::Iso { r: 470Ω ± 1%, c: 100nF ± 10% };  // the engine checks this against the netlist
    }
    port output {
        accepts Load::Sampled { r_sw: ..=1kΩ, c_s: ..=8pF, f_s: ..=50kHz, t_s: 1.125us.., prev: 0V..=3.4V, leak: ±1uA };
        presents z_out: Iso { r: ..=480Ω, c: 90nF.. };              // what the ADC sees driving it
    }
    let h = ac(output.v / input.v);
    let lsb = 3.3V / 4096;
    spec f_c:   h.f_high(-3dB) in 3.39kHz ± 15%;
    spec alias: (h.at(1kHz) / h.at(25kHz)).db() >= 15dB;             // [EE] 17.7 dB
    /// The ADC's sampling capacitor grabs charge each sample; the RC must refill it within the sample time.
    spec drives_adc: tran(output.sample_error()).window(1ms..=2ms).max() <= 1 * lsb;   // [EE] −0.47 LSB
}
```

`drives_adc` needs no bench. The `Load::Sampled` shape lowers to the switch, the 8 pF capacitor reset to `prev` and the 50 kHz clock, so **the load itself is the stimulus**. This is [EE] §2.6's point: the ADC is a dynamic load that only a transient with the sample clock can see. Here it is part of what the output accepts, so no AC-only check can accidentally stand in for it.

### 2.6 `AdcInput`: a model block

```rust
/// STM32F103 ADC input (DS5319 §5.3.18). No circuit: the contract's `presents` *are* the model.
pub block AdcInput { port input: Analog<In>; port vref: Power<In>; port gnd: Ground; }

contract AdcInput {
    #[model]
    port input {
        /// The R_AIN rule for 12 bits at t_s = 1.125 µs, or a reservoir capacitor ≥ 20 × C_s (Walsh 2012).
        accepts Source { z_src: ..=13.5kΩ } | Source { z_src: Iso { c: 160pF.. } };   // [C] 1.125 µs / (8 pF · ln 2^14) − 1 kΩ
        presents z_in: Load::Sampled { r_sw: ..=1kΩ, c_s: ..=8pF, f_s: 50kHz, t_s: 1.125us, prev: 0V..=vref.v, leak: ±1uA };
    }
    #[model]
    port vref {
        accepts Supply { v: 3.3V ± 3% };
        presents i: ..=200uA;                                       // an assumption: the datasheet row isn't in [EE]
    }
    pub let code = 4096 * input.v / vref.v;                         // a measure the parent can use
    pub let lsb  = vref.v / 4096;
}
```

A `#[model]` port has no circuit behind it. The engine lowers its `presents` to SPICE (a switched capacitor here). This is the IBIS idea: publish the port, not the transistors. It also removes the need for a separate "ADC input model" block in the driver's bench. Any bench whose load is `Load::Model<AdcInput>`, or a `Load::Sampled` that contains it, uses the same numbers.

### 2.7 `McuLoad`: a model block that emits an event

```rust
/// Stands in for the MCU's digital supply current.
pub block McuLoad { port vdd: Power<In>; port gnd: Ground; }

contract McuLoad {
    #[model]
    port vdd {
        accepts Supply { v: 2.0V..=3.6V };
        presents i: 5mA..=30mA below 1.8V: Off;     // the firmware's activity; below 1.8 V brown-out reset holds it off

    }
}
```

`below 1.8V: Off` gives the model's validity range. Without it, an ideal current sink keeps pulling 30 mA while the rail collapses in a fault, and the simulation drives the rail to −0.47 V **[S]**, which no real MCU does. The port monitors turn "model outside its valid range" into a tag, and the verdict becomes UNDECIDED instead of quietly using a false number.

    spec mcu_step_to_code: tran(code).deviation() <= 4 under mcu.vdd.wake;   // [EE] −0.4 / +2.1; −10 / +12 without the filter
    spec droop_to_code:    tran(code).deviation() <= 4 under vbus.droop;     // [EE] 2047.9–2048.3
    spec alias_rejection:  (k.at(1kHz) / k.at(25kHz)).db() >= 15dB;          // [EE] 17.7 dB
    spec t_valid: tran(code).settles(2048 ± 4).since(vbus.attach) <= 1.5s under vbus.attach;   // [EE] 2048.05 at 1.49 s
    #[warn]
    spec inrush: tran(vbus.i).max() <= 1A under vbus.attach;          // informative: [EE] 424 mA; real edges need cable L

    // usb_cap (vbus.requires.c) and usb_current (vbus.requires.i) are generated by Usb2LowPower: no line here.

    // ── the fault: the USB cable is pulled while running ────────────────────────────────────────────
    /// The power-fail warning: VBUS below 4.0 V, read by a comparator on a VBUS divider.
    let warning = tran(vbus.v).crossing(falling 4.0V);
    /// The first ADC reading that is wrong by more than 4 codes.
    let bad = tran((code - 2048).abs()).crossing(rising 4);
    /// The firmware needs 50 µs between the warning and the first bad reading, to stop sampling.
    #[confidence(worst_case)]
    spec warn_lead: bad - warning >= 50us during vbus.unplug where vbus.unplug.for == 5ms;      // [S] ≥ 103.0 µs
    /// The rail must stay up long enough to save state.

    #[confidence(worst_case)]
    spec recover: tran((code - 2048).abs()).at(1s) <= 4 after vbus.unplug;                       // [S] 0.10 codes worst of 3
    // vbus.reversed: no spec written. Every part's abs-max and derating checks run inside every fault.

### 2.10 What the fault shows, and a reversed connector

**The unplug fault.** The cable opens at t₀ from the steady state. I simulated the eight combinations of VBUS ∈ {4.40, 5.25 V}, I_mcu ∈ {5, 30 mA} and C_bus ∈ {4.23, 5.17 µF} (±10%), with nominal parts elsewhere and the MCU sink folding back below 1.8 V **[S]**:

| VBUS | I_mcu | C_bus | VBUS < 4.0 V (warning) | reading off by > 4 codes | v3 < 3.0 V | warn_lead | holdup |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.40 V | 30 mA | 4.23 µF | 55.6 µs | 160.8 µs | 244.7 µs | 105.2 µs | **244.7 µs** |
| 4.40 V | 30 mA | 5.17 µF | 67.8 µs | 193.8 µs | 291.0 µs | 126.0 µs | 291.0 µs |
| 5.25 V | 30 mA | 4.23 µF | 175.2 µs | 278.2 µs | 364.2 µs | **103.0 µs** | 364.2 µs |
| 5.25 V | 30 mA | 5.17 µF | 213.9 µs | 337.5 µs | 437.0 µs | 123.6 µs | 437.0 µs |
| 4.40 / 5.25 V | 5 mA | either | 329–401 µs, or later | beyond 600 µs | beyond 600 µs | passes (Beyond, passing side) | passes |

Why the reading goes bad at all: the ADC measures against its own rail (`code = 4096 · v_ain / va`). When the rail falls, the signal at mid-scale doesn't fall with it at once, because the mid-rail bias follows the rail through a 50 ms RC. So the code *rises*, by 58 codes before the rail even reaches 3.2 V **[S]**. The worst `warn_lead` sits at the **highest** VBUS, which you might not guess. More stored charge above 4.0 V delays the warning, while the reading goes bad about 100 µs after the regulator drops out in every case. Both specs pass with margin (103 µs ≥ 50 µs; 245 µs ≥ 150 µs). `recover`: after a 5 ms dropout at 30 mA the error is 70 codes at 100 ms, 7.2 at 500 ms and **0.10 at 1 s**. After a 300 µs blip, the rail never falls below 3.11 V and the error is 0.16 codes at 100 ms **[S]**. A real engine run adds every part tolerance and the dropout length as knobs (§4.4).

**The reversed connector.** With VBUS and ground swapped, the LDO's VIN pin goes negative at once. Its absolute maximum is −0.3 V, so the automatic abs-max check FAILs, with no spec written. The magnitude isn't trustworthy: the behavioral LDO isn't valid in reverse, and ngspice reports the VBUS pin at −1.6 V with an absurd 36 A **[S]**. So the port monitor tags the rest of the run `model-out-of-range`. The FAIL itself doesn't depend on the model, because any pass transistor with a body diode conducts in reverse.

The fix an agent would propose is a series Schottky diode. Composition then catches its cost **before any simulation**. The diode drops about 0.23 V at 30 mA, so the supply that `ldo.vin` sees is roughly 4.17 V open-circuit, and `Ldo3v3` accepts only 4.3 V and up. The connection check fails with both lines shown:

```
error[E-accepts]: `ldo.vin` accepts Supply.v 4.3V..=5.5V, but receives ≈ 4.17V..=5.02V
 --> sensor_board.spl:9:17
   = note: Usb2LowPower.v 4.40V..=5.25V, minus d_rev's forward drop 0.23V at 30 mA (characterized)
   = help: the LDO needs 13 mV of headroom at 30 mA [EE]; widen `Ldo3v3.vin accepts v` to 3.6V..=5.5V
           and re-check its own specs (4 new corners per DC setup), or use a P-FET ideal diode
```

Simulated with the diode at the worst corner (4.40 V, 0.5 Ω, 30 mA): the LDO input is 4.155 V and the rail still regulates at 3.2964 V **[S]**. So the diagnosis is right: the LDO's declared range is narrower than it needs to be.

**A correction to [EE] §4.5 found while writing `MidRef`.** Two ±1% resistors in a divider move its ratio by ±1%, not ±0.5% (0.5 × (1% + 1%)). The midscale worst case is therefore **−27.2 / +26.4 codes**, a `worst_case` FAIL of ±20. At the default `sigma(3)` it is about ±16.5 codes and passes **[C]**. The board above says `#[confidence(worst_case)]` on `midscale` to make that visible. The designer either accepts sigma(3) or uses 0.1% divider resistors.

## 3. Hierarchy

### 3.1 How a child's specs and setups travel

```
  GainStage (definition)                                    SensorBoard (placement `amp`)
  ──────────────────────                                    ─────────────────────────────
  port vdd    accepts Supply{…}   presents i ≤ 200 µA   ──►   net va:  checked against vdda.vout (both ways)
  port input  accepts Source{…}   presents z_in ≥ 450k  ──►   port sensor: board's own accepts ⊆ amp's accepts
  port output accepts Iso|Rc      presents z_out Rl     ──►   net oa:  checked against aaf.input (both ways)
  spec gain, f_low, f_high,                             ──►   board.amp.gain … : verdicts carried, resting on
       swing, psrr, stable                                    the connection checks (rung 1), or re-run in context
  derived setups + run tables                           ──►   stay home; keyed by GainStage's block key
```

- **Specs travel with a path** (`board.amp.gain`, the `SpecPath` of next_synthesis M1). Each keeps its standalone verdict and gets an in-context status: *carried* (its accepted environment is proven to hold at this placement), *composed* (a parent measure evaluated from cached tables), or *re-run in context* (a flat check at the parent's knobs).
- **Setups stay home.** The child's derived setups are its unit tests, never re-run because it's placed (refs §18.1 item 9: "checkers travel, stimulus stays home").
- **`accepts` become checks at every net the port touches.** **`presents` become the numbers those checks use** for the other direction.
- **Lifting is automatic.** The board's own `vbus` and `sensor` ports carry the children's needs outward. `vbus presents c` is the sum of the children's `presents c` on that net plus `c_bus`, and `vbus presents i` is the LDO's input current. If the board is ever placed in a bigger system, those are its published numbers. For its own `accepts`, the board may be narrower than the children's, never wider (checked).

### 3.2 Proving the child's assumptions, both directions

At every net, the engine builds a **net environment** (edg's "link", [refs] §14.2) from the one driver's `presents` and the receivers' `presents`, then checks four kinds of containment:

| Direction | Check | Example on the board |
|---|---|---|
| driver → receiver: level | driver `presents v` ⊆ each receiver's `accepts Supply.v` / `Source.signal` | `va`: 3.224–3.366 V ⊆ amp 3.201–3.399 V ✓ |
| driver → receiver: impedance | driver `presents z_out` ⊆ receiver `accepts z_src` | `oa`: amp Rl{r ≤ 0.1 Ω, l ≤ 0.55 mH} ⊆ aaf Rl{r ≤ 10 Ω, l ≤ 1 mH} ✓ |
| receiver → driver: load | Σ receivers' `presents i`, `c`, `z_in` ⊆ driver's `accepts Load` | `v3`: mcu 5–30 mA + vdda ≤ 1 mA ⊆ ldo 0.1–50 mA ✓ |
| receiver → driver: events | each receiver's `emits` ⊆ some `event` the driver accepts | `v3`: mcu `wake` 5 → 30 mA/1 µs ⊆ ldo `load_step` ✓ |

**The gain stage in the board, net by net** ([C] from the declared numbers):

| Net | Direction | Presented | Accepted | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `va` | vdda → amp.vdd | v = v3 − (0…10.1 mV) = 3.224–3.366 V; z_out ≤ 10.2 Ω | v 3.201–3.399 V; z_src ≤ 20 Ω | ✓ ✓ |
| `va` | vdda → adc.vref | same | v 3.201–3.399 V | ✓ |
| `va` | amp.vdd + adc.vref → vdda | i ≤ 200 µA + 200 µA = 400 µA | i ≤ 1 mA | ✓ |
| `sensor` | board.sensor → amp.input | z_src 100 Ω–10 kΩ; Sine ≤ 100 mV, 20 Hz–1 kHz | z_src ≤ 10 kΩ; Sine ≤ 100 mV, 20 Hz–1 kHz | ✓ ✓ |
| `oa` | amp.output → aaf.input | z_out Rl{≤ 0.1 Ω, ≤ 0.55 mH} | z_src Rl{≤ 10 Ω, ≤ 1 mH} | ✓ |
| `oa` | aaf.input → amp.output | z_in Iso{465–475 Ω, 90–110 nF} | Iso{400 Ω–1 kΩ, ≤ 220 nF} | ✓ |
| `oa` | level | amp presents 0.1–3.2 V | aaf accepts any level | ✓ |

All pass, so `board.amp.gain`, `f_low`, `f_high`, `swing`, `psrr` and `stable` are **carried** with the verdicts from GainStage's own run tables. Each record lists these rows in `rests_on`. **No run is needed.**

**How containment is decided (the honest part).** Containment is interval inclusion *per field* when both sides use the same shape: `Iso` against `Iso`, `Rl` against `Rl`, `R` against `R`. A few cross-shape rules are exact: an `R` is an `Rl` with l = 0, and a bare `Load { r, c }` with c = 0 is an `R`. When the shapes differ with no rule (an op-amp output described by `z_out: ..=5Ω at 1kHz` against a filter that accepts `Rl`), the check is **UNDECIDED at rung 1**. The engine then climbs the ladder of [H] §4.6: it composes the two cached port tables (rung 3), or it simulates in context (rung 4). A scalar impedance can't stand in for a function of frequency. [H] §2.3 showed a scalar-range method miss a chain corner by 1% with its check passing. That is why `presents z_out` is written as a shape over a band, and why the same numbers read differently in context:

- `AaFilter.f_c` checked over its own accepted source, `Rl{r ≤ 10 Ω, l ≤ 1 mH}`, spans **2.99–3.91 kHz**, above the ±15% limit of 3.90 kHz. With an ideal source it would be 3.05–3.80 kHz **[C]**. So the standalone verdict is FAIL (C_aa at −10% plus the inductive source). The ideal-source default bench of v0.1 would have called it PASS. The fix is C_aa at ±5% (≈ 3.7 kHz top) or a ±20% limit. In context the board's `alias_rejection` is what matters, and it is checked on its own.
- `Ldo3v3.presents z_out … where vout.i >= 5mA`: a `where` on a `presents` is itself an obligation. At `v3`, the receivers present i ≥ 5 mA (the MCU's floor), so the condition is discharged and the guarantee holds in context. That is [EE] §3.1's "the range shrinks in context", done by the checker instead of by a person.

### 3.3 Shared nets and ambient knobs are joined, not added

A net has **one** value per run, whichever receiver reads it. So its value is a **join key** across the children on it, like `temp` ([H] §1.4). On the board, `va` feeds both the mid-rail bias and the ADC's reference. The design is ratiometric: V_REF errors cancel. The table shows what happens to `midscale` at rung 1 if each child took `va` from the range independently **[C]**:

| Method | midscale range | vs ±20 |
|---|---|---|
| `va` independent per receiver (vmid from one va, ADC reference from another) | 1903.0 – 2202.4 | can't prove anything |
| `va` joined (one value per corner) | 2020.8 – 2074.4 (−27.2 / +26.4) | exact worst case |

The join is automatic in this option because the parent builds a net environment, not a list of pairwise checks. Every receiver's `vdd.v` knob, `vref.v` knob and so on becomes one **bound** knob in the parent: the net's value, a function of the driver's knobs.

### 3.4 Reusing cached child results

**The key** (a refinement of [H] §4.3):

```
BlockKey(GainStage) = fnv64(
    the elaborated definition, children included (MidRef), with params applied
  + part models and part records (the Mcp6001 subcircuit text hash, its record revision)
  + the setup shapes: which environment members exist, their shapes, the excitation, the events
  + engine settings + backend identity ("ngspice-42, KLU")                       )
NOT in the key:  the placement path; the neighbours; the numeric ranges inside `accepts`
Rows:            keyed by the physical values of the setup's knobs (engine_types T6)
Tables:          .spicy/blocks/<key>/<setup>.runs   and   .spicy/blocks/<key>/abcd.<ports>.bin
```

Ranges are out of the key on purpose. Rows are keyed by physical knob values, so narrowing `output accepts c: ..=220nF` to `..=150nF` reuses every row still inside, and widening it adds only the new corners.

| Edit | GainStage runs | Board work |
|---|---|---|
| a comment, a doc line, the board's layout | 0 | 0 (spans aren't in `rev`) |
| `aaf.c_aa` 100 nF → 68 nF | 0 | AaFilter's own setups (5-knob cones: 32 runs each); re-check `oa` and `ain` (arithmetic, 0 runs); board AC specs recomposed from tables (AaFilter's table rebuilt, 2 runs per corner); board transients whose cone holds `aaf.*` re-run |
| widen `GainStage.output accepts c` to `..=470nF` | the new corners only (half the setup's rows) | carried verdicts re-derived; the `oa` check re-run (0 runs) |
| a GainStage internal edit (R_f) | its setups (new key) | its carried verdicts replaced; board specs through `amp` recomposed |
| the Mcp6001 model or record revision | its setups (new key) | same |
| `Usb2LowPower` library version | 0 | every board setup that uses `vbus` becomes `stale` |

**Characterized tables.** For linear AC, the table is a per-corner ABCD matrix over frequency between two ports. For power-supply rejection it is a 3×3 matrix including `vdd` ([H] §6 q. 5), two or three runs per corner. For DC, it is the port's V–I table over the supply axis, solved to a fixed point in the parent ([H] H3). A parent measure such as `code_gain` becomes matrix products: the sensor's R_s, GainStage's table, AaFilter's table, AdcInput's model load. They are joined on `temp` and on the net `va`, which carries the ratio `code = 4096 · v_ain / va`. [H] measured this to be exact to 1e-14 on linear AC.

### 3.5 When flat simulation is still required

| Situation | On the reference board | Why the ports can't decide it |
|---|---|---|
| Board-only specs about a **shared rail, in time** | `mcu_step_to_code`, `droop_to_code`, `t_valid`, `warn_lead`, `holdup`, `recover` | The step travels LDO → v3 → VDDA filter → both the reference and the signal path at once. Its effect on the code is a race between two paths. A port table of each block is small-signal and frequency-domain; the transient board needs the circuit |
| **A FAIL** from composition | any | Composed bounds prove PASS only; one flat run at the composed worst corner confirms the counterexample ([H] §4.4) |
| **A containment check fails or is UNDECIDED** | `oa` if the amp only declared a scalar z_out | Rung 3 (tables) if both children have them, rung 4 otherwise |
| **Large-signal port behaviour** | `swing` into the real load; the ADC kick while the op-amp slews | Impedance shapes are small-signal. IBIS-like V–I tables are the later path |
| **A propagated guarantee outside its table** | `vdda.presents v` when the MCU draws more than accepted | Extrapolation; the check fails first |
| **Coupling that doesn't go through a port** | LDO self-heating near the op-amp (not modeled) | Not a port quantity. A block may say `#[in_context]` |
| **Faults** | every fault setup | A fault drives ports outside what anyone accepts, by definition |

## 4. What the engine does with it

### 4.1 The lowering

```
 contract (ports: accepts / presents / event / fault / emits; specs; benches)
   │  resolve environments: named items + overrides + type defaults → EnvResolution per port
   ▼
 setup derivation: per spec, the tuple (block, static env, excitation, events, analysis) → SetupId
   │  specs with equal tuples share a setup; each `presents` gets its standard characterization setup
   ▼
 per setup: an EngineDeck (DUT subcircuit + environment elements) + knob bindings + Needs + PortMonitors
   │
   ├─ block level: Plan per block (its sides, setups, knobs) ─► check() as engine_types §3, per setup
   └─ parent level: NetEnv per net ─► ConnectionChecks ─► ChildUse { rung } per placement
                     composed measures read child tables (join on ambient + net keys)
                     board-only specs ─► flat setups of the parent
```

### 4.2 Setups become decks

What each environment member lowers to:

| Member | SPICE elements | Knobs |
|---|---|---|
| `Supply { v, z_src }` | `V` source + series `R` (or `R` + `L` for `Rl`) | `port.v`, `port.z_src` (Interface) |
| `Supply.ripple` (under it) | `AC 1` on the same `V`; the result is scaled by `vpp` | none (the band is the measure's axis) |
| `Source { signal: Sine, z_src }` | `V` with `AC 1` (AC setups) or `SIN(…)` (tran) + series impedance | `port.z_src`; in tran also `signal.amp`, `signal.freq` |
| `Load { r, c, i }` | `R`, `C`, `I` in parallel | `port.load.r/c/i` |
| `Load::Iso { r, c }` | `R` then `C` to ground | two knobs |
| `Load::Sampled` | a smooth switch (`R_sw`), `C_s` with a reset source to `prev`, a PULSE clock | `prev`, `leak` (sink ±1 µA) |
| `Step`, `Ramp` event | `PWL` on the source or sink; start at t₀ = 100 µs after the operating point (printed) | only fields written as ranges |
| `Open` fault | ngspice smooth switch (`SW` with hysteresis, or `PS vswitch`) in series, driven by `PWL` | `for` if a range |
| `Swap` fault | the source re-wired with opposite polarity (a separate deck) | none |
| `presents z_out` | excitation off; 1 A AC injected at the port | the setup's environment knobs |
| `presents z_in` | voltage-driven; current read through a 0 V probe source | same |
| **Port monitors** (every setup) | read `v(port)` and `i(port)`; compare with `accepts`, part abs-max, model validity | none |

### 4.3 How knobs are affected

| Kind | Example | In the child's own setups | In the parent | Correlation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ambient** (`accepts temp`) | `temp` | a range knob | the **same** knob; the child's range must contain the parent's | fully shared: a join key |
| **Part, per placement** | `amp.r_f.value`, `ldo.u1.vref` | statistical knobs; the rows of the child's table | one per placement, independent (E16); a `lot` ties them | independent |
| **Interface, on an internal net** | `amp.vdd.v`, `amp.output.load.r`, `aaf.input.z_src.l` | range knobs, origin `Interface` | **bound**: replaced by the net's value (a function of the driver's knobs); gone as knobs | the net is a join key for all its receivers |
| **Interface, on the parent's own port** | `board.vbus.v`, `board.vbus.z_src`, `board.sensor.z_src` | — | range knobs of the parent (from its `accepts`) | shared by everything on that net |
| **Presented range** (a child's activity) | `mcu.vdd.i` 5–30 mA | — (the child's own presents) | a range knob of the parent, owned by `mcu` | per placement |
| **Bench-local / event** | step size, edge, t₀ | point values: not knobs | not visible | — |
| **Event range** | `vbus.unplug.for` 10 µs–5 ms | a range knob of that setup only | only in that setup | — |
| **Choice / mode** | `amp.output` load `Iso | Rc`; a Run/Sleep mode | one setup per alternative (or `KnobKind::Mode`) | discharged: the actual load picks one | — |
| **Fault** | `vbus.unplug`, `vbus.reversed` | one setup per fault (a scenario) | per fault; never combined unless a spec names two | — |

A useful consequence: the engine can prune knobs from the *shape's meaning*, not only from the netlist. A pin-to-pin ratio `v(out)/v(in)` measured at the driven port doesn't depend on that port's source impedance. For a linear two-port, V₁/V₂ = A + B/Z_L whatever drives port 1. So `input.z_src` drops out of `gain`'s cone. Call it the **source rule**, next to the capacitor rule of engine_types §2.3.

### 4.4 How many runs, roughly

Enumeration is 2^|cone| per setup. Costs are ~1 ms for an op or AC run in library mode, and 40–70 ms for a board transient (67 ms for the MCU-step transient in batch mode, including process start-up; 10–30 ms for the 1.6 s `t_valid` and `recover` transients) **[S]**. temp isn't modeled in these behavioral parts, so it is tagged `not_modeled` and kept out of the cones.

| Where | Setups | Largest cone | Runs (order) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ldo3v3 | env/dc, env/ac(vin), presents z_out, 3 events, dropout_row | 7 (z_out: +1-D search along `vout.i`, log) | ≈ 350 AC/DC + ≈ 200 tran | the z_out peak is at the lightest accepted load; the search is E1's |
| VddaFilter | env/ac, presents | 4 | ≈ 50 | |
| GainStage | env × 2 load shapes, presents z_in / z_out, psrr, step_test × 2, swing | **13** (9 parts + vdd.v, vdd.z_src, load r, load c) | 2 × 8,192 AC if enumerated; **over the M3 budget of 2^12** | needs the loop (E4) or a sensitivity screen; the load `c` axis needs an interior search (phase dip, [EE] §3.3) |
| AaFilter | env/ac, sampled/tran | 6 | ≈ 128 + 64 tran | |
| AdcInput, McuLoad | none (models) | — | 0 | |
| Board, rung 1 (checks) | — | — | **0** | about 20 containment rows, arithmetic |
| Board, rung 3 (`code_gain`, `alias`, `ripple_to_code`) | composed | — | table builds: 2–3 runs per child corner, once | then 0 per edit elsewhere |
| Board, `midscale` | env/dc | 7 (divider, V_OS, leak, va chain) | 128 op | or arithmetic (E8) |
| Board transients | wake, droop, attach, unplug, reversed | 14–20 | over budget if enumerated; with the loop ≈ 200–400 each × 40 ms ≈ 10–20 s each | the flat-only specs of §3.5 |

Total, roughly: **a few thousand block runs** (seconds), **zero runs for carried verdicts**, and **about a minute of board transients** on one worker. Most of that minute is fault and event setups. A flat check of the whole board would have a cone of 25+ knobs on every AC side, beyond enumeration.

## 5. Escape hatches, and how they stay checked

| Hatch | Written as | How it stays checked |
|---|---|---|
| **A datasheet row** | `where vin.v == 4.3V, vout.i == 50mA` | Must lie inside `accepts` (compile-time error otherwise). The other knobs still vary |
| **A one-off setup** | `bench name { overrides, ..env }` | `..env` keeps the rest of the environment. Leaving it needs `#[outside(reason)]`. Port monitors run in every run. Outside results are tagged `characterization` and never used for composition |
| **A setup that changes the circuit** (loop gain, a CMRR drive) | `bench loop { inject: u1.fb, ..env }`; a differential `Source::Diff { dm, cm }` covers CMRR as `ac(out / input.cm)` | same as above. The injection point is a named internal net, so a rename is a compile error |
| **A raw SPICE fixture** | `bench vendor_tb = spice("benches/tlv755p_eval.cir", dut: "XU1") { ports: { vin: "IN", vout: "OUT", gnd: "0" }, params: { VIN: vin.v } };` | (1) The DUT line is replaced by our exported subcircuit, so the fixture tests *this* design. (2) The port map must name every port (compile error otherwise). (3) Port monitors read `v`/`i` at the mapped nodes and compare them with `accepts`. (4) The file's hash is in `rev`. (5) Measures come from our measure language on mapped probes; the engine never reads `.meas` from logs. (6) Mapped params become knobs; unmapped ones are printed as fixed |
| **A contract-only block** | `#[model]` ports | Its `presents` are its behaviour. With a circuit behind the same ports (P4), the circuit's own setups check the model's claims: the same contract is the equivalence criterion (G11) |
| **A known failure / waiver** | `#[expect(fail, reason)]`, `#[allow(…)]` | Tracked, as in v0.1 |

## 6. Gap coverage

| Gap | This option | How |
|---|---|---|
| G1 no spec home | **improves** | `presents` gives port guarantees a home; `requires` turns a standard's limits into specs automatically |
| G2 limit duplicated | **improves** (partly) | a standard's numbers live once, in its `environment` item, referenced by name; `#[req(…)]` path for external IDs |
| G3 benches not reusable | **improves** | the bench is derived from the environment, so it can't go stale. Named environments are reused by every block with a matching port (the cross-block reuse gap_map marks "Not") |
| G4 bench outside assumptions | **improves** | by construction for derived setups; `#[outside]` for one-offs; port monitors at run time, for raw fixtures too; the coverage list of unexercised members |
| G5 corners explode | **worse**, path | every accepted range adds a knob to block setups (GainStage: 13). The source rule, sensitivity screens and the loop (E4) are the path |
| G6 worst case is a guess | same engine; **improves** loads | load ranges are real knobs with log axes and interior searches, not "full load" |
| G7 distributions | neutral | engine policy, unchanged |
| G8 no correlation | **improves** across blocks | net environments are join keys (`va`: 1903–2202 → 2021–2074 codes) |
| G9 MC cost | neutral | engine |
| G10 sub-blocks trusted | **improves** | both-way containment per net, including events; carried / composed / simulated statuses with `rests_on` |
| G11 model drift | **path** | `#[model]` and circuit share one port contract; checking one against the other is P4 |
| G12 IP handoff | **improves** | a block ships its port contract + verdicts + tables: a Liberty-like handoff. Encrypted IP: path (value-only backend) |
| G13 traceability | partial | `SpecPath`, named environments with versions in `rev`; no requirement import yet |
| G14 no "not tested" state | **improves** | environment coverage; `characterization` and `model-out-of-range` tags; UNDECIDED at unmatched shapes |
| G15 vendor models silent | partial | model validity ranges on ports turn silent extrapolation into a tag; `#[declared(datasheet)]` presents |
| G16 datasheet data | partial | provenance on declared `presents`; extraction quality untouched |
| G17 diffs / CI | partial | text, grouped by port; but a library environment change is a diff elsewhere (mitigated by `stale` + `spicy env --diff`) |
| G18 AI single points | **improves** | every claim prints its environment; the agent edits one structured field per fact |
| G19 faults | **improves** at ports; **path** inside | faults come with interface types (USB: unplug, reversed) with `during` / `after` scoping, abs-max inside every fault, and a per-port FMEA table (port × fault × spec). Faults of internal parts (a capacitor shorting, a bent pin between two internal nets) aren't port events and need `fault` on parts and nets |

**Where this option is weak:**
1. **Shape matching.** Containment is exact only when both sides speak the same shape. A mismatch climbs to tables or simulation. That is sound but less magical, and the shape library must grow carefully (switching regulators, transmission lines, nonlinear clamps).
2. **Heavier block benches.** Every accepted range is a knob. A careful author who declares everything pays in cones (G5).
3. **Board-only specs gain nothing special.** The six transient board specs on shared rails are ordinary flat checks, with the same cost as in any option.
4. **Indirection.** Named environments and type defaults are one more place where meaning can hide. §1.5's rules mitigate it, but a reader of a diff can still miss a library change.
5. **A mini-language for propagated guarantees** (`vin.v - (0mV..=10.1mV)`, `below 1.8V: Off`) needs its own rules and error messages.

## 7. Strengths and weaknesses by situation

| Situation | How it goes | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Big boards** | **shines** | Most block reuse is decided by arithmetic at nets (0 runs). Linear board specs compose from tables. Only the shared-rail transients go flat |
| **Quick experiments** | **fine** | An undeclared port gets the ideal environment, and `where` pins a value. But "try it with a 1 kΩ load" means editing the environment or a one-line `bench`, a small ceremony |
| **Library IP** | **shines** | The port contract is the datasheet header and pin table in checkable form. A user can't place it outside its accepted environment without a diagnostic |
| **AI editing** | **good** | One fact, one field, on the port it concerns, from a small typed vocabulary. Fixes are local ("widen `vin accepts v`"), and the checker explains both sides. The risk: an agent "fixing" a FAIL by widening what a block accepts. So the report shows requirement edits separately from design edits (agent_flows G6) |
| **A novice user** | **mixed** | "What does this input accept? What does this output drive?" is a question an engineer understands at once, and the std environments (USB, logic families) give right answers for free. But a novice must learn that a load belongs on the output and a source on the input, and what a shape is |
| **The editor's form UI** | **shines** | Each port is a card with **Accepts**, **Events / Faults** and **Presents** rows, which is a datasheet laid out as a form. Derived setups appear as read-only tabs; a `bench` is a form of overrides |
| **Diffs** | **good**, one catch | A range change is one field on one line. The catch is inherited meaning (profiles, type defaults), handled by the formatter writing names out, by versioning and by `stale` |
| **Circuit-changing tests** (loop gain, PSRR of an internal node) | **hurts a little** | The environment can't express a broken loop. These are `bench`es, as in any option |
| **Faults** | **good at ports** | Cable, connector and supply faults come with the interface type. Internal part faults need the path |

## 8. Open questions

1. **The shape library.** Which impedance and load shapes ship in v1 (`R`, `Rl`, `Iso`, `Sampled`, `Model`)? Is there a generic "impedance mask over frequency" as a last resort?
2. **Events as ranges.** Should `Step { edge: 1us.. }` mean "every edge at least this slow" (a knob), or "the fastest is worst" (an assumption the engine must then check)?
3. **Default environments for `Power` and `Analog`.** None (as proposed), or a lint-level default such as ±5%?
4. **`presents` from netlist extraction.** For passive blocks (AaFilter's `z_in` is exactly an Iso), should the engine write the `presents` itself, and the author only confirm it?
5. **Combined events.** Should `under a + b` (a droop during an MCU step) be allowed, and how does the run count grow?
6. **Internal faults.** `fault` on parts and nets (open, short, drift) with the same `during` / `after` scoping, or a separate FMEA item?
